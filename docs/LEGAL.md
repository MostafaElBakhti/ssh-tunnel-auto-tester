## Legal Considerations

### By Jurisdiction

#### United States
- The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) prohibits unauthorized access
- Testing on your own systems or with explicit permission is generally legal
- Bypassing carrier restrictions may violate service agreements

#### European Union
- GDPR considerations for data in transit
- Various national computer crime laws apply
- Authorization required for network testing

#### United Kingdom
- Computer Misuse Act applies
- Unauthorized access is a criminal offense
- Proper authorization required

#### Other Jurisdictions
- Laws vary significantly by country
- Research local regulations before use
- When in doubt, obtain legal advice

### What is Generally Legal

✅ Testing on your own servers and networks
✅ Testing with explicit written permission
✅ Security research on isolated test environments
✅ Educational purposes in controlled settings

### What May Be Illegal

❌ Testing without authorization
❌ Accessing systems you don't own
❌ Bypassing security measures without permission
❌ Violating terms of service
❌ Using to access paid content for free

## Ethical Use Guidelines

### Acceptable Uses

1. **Personal Infrastructure Testing**
   - Test connectivity to your own SSH servers
   - Verify your security configurations
   - Troubleshoot network issues

2. **Authorized Security Testing**
   - Penetration testing with written permission
   - Security audits for organizations
   - Compliance verification

3. **Educational Research**
   - Learning about network protocols
   - Understanding tunneling techniques
   - Academic research projects

4. **Legitimate Remote Access**
   - Accessing your own systems while traveling
   - Connecting to authorized corporate resources
   - Technical troubleshooting

### Unacceptable Uses

1. **Unauthorized Access**
   - Breaking into systems you don't own
   - Bypassing security without permission
   - Accessing restricted networks

2. **Fraud/Theft**
   - Obtaining free data by bypassing limits
   - Circumventing paid services
   - Financial gain through unauthorized means

3. **Malicious Activities**
   - Launching attacks through tunnels
   - Hiding illegal activities
   - Evading law enforcement

4. **Privacy Violations**
   - Intercepting others' traffic
   - Man-in-the-middle attacks
   - Data theft

## Best Practices

### Before Use

1. **Document Authorization**
   - Keep written permission records
   - Note authorized scope and dates
   - Record who authorized the testing

2. **Understand the Environment**
   - Know what you're connecting to
   - Understand network topology
   - Be aware of monitoring systems

3. **Have an Exit Strategy**
   - Know how to disconnect safely
   - Understand logging implications
   - Plan for incident response

### During Use

1. **Stay Within Scope**
   - Only test authorized systems
   - Respect access limitations
   - Don't exceed permission boundaries

2. **Minimize Impact**
   - Avoid disrupting services
   - Use reasonable timeouts/delays
   - Don't overwhelm target systems

3. **Maintain Records**
   - Log your activities
   - Document findings
   - Keep evidence of authorization

### After Use

1. **Clean Up**
   - Close all connections
   - Remove temporary files
   - Clear sensitive data

2. **Report Findings**
   - Share results with stakeholders
   - Document any issues found
   - Recommend improvements

3. **Review Logs**
   - Check for unexpected activity
   - Verify proper disconnect
   - Look for errors

## Responsible Disclosure

If you discover security vulnerabilities while using this tool:

1. **Do Not Exploit**
   - Don't use vulnerabilities for unauthorized access
   - Don't share publicly without coordination

2. **Report Responsibly**
   - Contact the affected organization
   - Give reasonable time to fix
   - Follow coordinated disclosure practices

3. **Document Properly**
   - Keep records of discovery
   - Note steps to reproduce
   - Maintain confidentiality until fixed
